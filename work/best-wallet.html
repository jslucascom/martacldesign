A zero-friction crypto converter built to rank and convert

Best Wallet needed a real-time crypto converter calculator. It had to be a tool people would actually use, a page that could bring in traffic from Google, and a gentle first step towards downloading the app and opening an account. I designed it end to end.

Role
Product Designer
Scope
UX/UI, SEO-driven Design
Tools
Figma

Context

Best Wallet is a crypto wallet product, competing in a space where trust, speed, and clarity are everything. Most people landing on the site come from Google. They know what they want, but they don't trust you yet. They want their answer fast, and they need to feel the numbers are right before they do anything with them.

While we were looking for ways to grow organic traffic, I suggested building our own converter. People search for these conversions all the time, and the pages ranking for them were either cluttered or almost empty. It felt like an easy place to do better.

Another part of the challenge was designing something that felt trustworthy enough to use for financial decisions, and simple enough that the primary conversion (calculator → download app → account creation) felt natural, not forced.

My Role

I owned this project end to end. I conducted competitor research, and then defined the feature scope and UX logic: which inputs it needed, how currencies behave, when rates refresh and in what order everything appears on the page. Finally, I designed the page and the calculator component with all its states, for desktop and mobile, and prepared everything for the developers.

The Opportunity & Goals

The page had to:

  • Be useful. If the calculator is slow or feels unreliable, nothing else on the page matters.
  • Rank on Google. Search engines need content they can index, but a wall of SEO text would make it feel forced.
  • Bring in sign-ups. The business needs accounts, but pushing too hard on a page about money kills trust straight away.

Most competitors picked one of these and sacrificed the other two. I wanted the order of the page to make each one help the next.

The User Flow

I designed the page around one simple journey. Someone searches "BTC to USD", lands on the page and gets their answer straight away. Everything else comes after that.

Right after the calculation, the three steps to download the app and create an account, because that's the moment people have just seen the tool works. Then the popular markets tables, for anyone who wants to check other pairs. And at the bottom, the FAQs, for people who still have questions before they decide.

Key decisions

  • The answer comes first. The calculator sits above the fold and works the moment the page loads. One input, one result, a swap button, the time of the last update and a single button. No banners and no sign-up wall. That small "last updated" feedback does a lot of work. In crypto, a number without a time next to it is a number you can't really trust.
  • A long list of currencies that still feels easy. The selector had to hold a lot of currencies. I split fiat and crypto with a clear divider, put search at the top and added icons, so people can spot their currency without reading every line. On mobile it opens as a bottom sheet, so everything stays within reach of your thumb.
  • SEO content people can actually use. Instead of long paragraphs written for Google, the SEO part of the page is two "popular markets" tables, crypto to fiat and fiat to crypto. Every pair in them can bring in visitors from search, and for someone already on the page they work as a quick reference.
  • Signing up as the natural next step. The three onboarding steps (create an account, connect a funding method, convert crypto) come right after the calculator. By the time people get there, they've already used the product. So the message reads more like "here's how to do this for real" than "give us your email".

The calculator, piece by piece

I designed the calculator as a self-contained module. The inputs, the currency dropdowns with icons, the swap toggle, the live rate, the refresh time and the button all live inside it, and the currency pairs can be configured. I designed and documented every state (default, hover, active, loading and empty), so the developers didn't have to guess.

What we shipped

  • A responsive page template with the calculator, the popular markets tables, the onboarding steps, FAQs and tutorials.
  • A reusable converter component with all its states documented.
  • All of it built on the existing Best Wallet design system, so it felt part of the product from day one.

The Results

The page was designed to serve three goals simultaneously: drive organic traffic through crypto conversion requests, provide genuine utility to users already on the site, and support the app download and account creation as the natural next step.

This translated into different results:

  • Organic traffic grew to 12% within 3 months
  • Average session time on calculator pages: 1 min 12 sec
  • 4.1% of calculator users went on to download the app
