Building a Brand System that Scales

ClickOut Media was growing fast, but it had two logos, two websites and no shared visual language. I led the rebrand as the only designer on the project: a new identity, a scalable design system the whole company could use, one website for partners and future hires, cross-product updates and a simpler way for affiliates to join the platform.

Role
Product & Brand Designer
Scope
Brand Strategy, Visual Identity, Design System, UX/UI.
Tools
Figma / Illustrator

At a glance

  • 2 brands and 2 websites became one
  • 1 design system, used by product, marketing and the wider team
  • Launched together: the new website and a new affiliate log in and sign up.

The Problem

ClickOut Media is an international affiliate marketing company. Its crypto, finance and tech publications reach over 50 million users a month.

When I joined, the company had two separate logos, two websites, and no unified visual language. This translated into a fragmented identity that didn't reflect the scale or ambition of the business. This created three compounding issues:

  • Trust: Partners and affiliates landing on different ClickOut products felt like they were dealing with different companies, which affected clarity and trust.
  • Speed: Designers kept solving the same problems again instead of working on new ones.
  • Scalability: With continuous growth and new products planned, the company couldn't afford to keep scaling this way.

The brief was to build one brand, bold enough to work everywhere: the website, the affiliate platform, social media, internal decks and everything in between.

The brief I gave myself was a bit different. Not "make it look consistent", but "build a system that survives". Something people would keep using after launch, without me checking every file.

My Role

I was the only designer on this project, from the first explorations to the rollout across the company. I made the final calls on the visual direction, and I worked closely with the Head of Design and the marketing and engineering leads.

I designed:

  • New identity and brand guidelines
  • A design system
  • The new website
  • Affiliate log in and sign up, and the update of the platform UI
  • The templates and assets the rest of the company used every day

A big part of the job was getting people on board. Some teams had worked without shared rules for years, so the new system had to be easier than what they were already doing.

The Strategy

The goal was to make the brand feel bolder, sharper and future-proofed. ClickOut looked like a small affiliate network. It needed to look like the international media company it already was.

  • One Logo, I cleaned up the logo to make it simpler and easier to read.
  • A modern and contemporary visual system with strong foundations and recognisable identity.
  • A Design System, easily scalable and with strong foundations.
  • Rules anyone can follow, aimed for everyone in the company, giving them the ability to translate any business requirement in line with our brand.

The Design System

Beyond the identity, the project required a full visual system that teams could use day to day.

The system was built to scale: clear enough that anyone on the team could apply it correctly, flexible enough to work across web, social, email and print. It included:

  • Foundations: colour tokens, variables, typography scale, spacing system, shadow and elevation scale.
  • Components: core components covering navigation, forms, data display, feedback states.
  • Patterns: page templates for the main product types we shipped.
  • Documentation: usage guidelines written for the different end users who will interact with our brand.
  • Library of visual assets: a large set to be used across our products, including custom iconography, illustrations, and graphic elements, so nobody had to make their own.

The website

Two websites became one. Partners and people looking for a job now land in the same place and get the same picture of what ClickOut is and does.

The navigation is split by audience: Affiliates, Advertisers, News, Careers and Contact. Each group finds its own path from the first click.

Careers got a big place on the new site. ClickOut was growing fast and hiring for many teams at once, so we needed a strong image as an employer too, not only as a partner. The Careers section shows what it's like to work at ClickOut and takes people straight to the open roles.

The pages use the full brand: bold colour, clear typography, custom illustrations and asymmetric layouts that give them a sense of movement.

Into the product: affiliate log in and sign up

ClickOut works with two kinds of partners. Affiliates use the platform to find offers and track their earnings. Advertisers don't use the platform at all: they start by talking to the team.

The old site didn't show that difference. One "Affiliates / Advertisers Log In" button sent both groups to the same log-in page. Advertisers had no account, and never would, but they had to go through that page to find a contact form. And affiliates who wanted to join found one long sign-up form, with every question on a single page. A lot to take in before you've even started.

I redesigned this entry point, with the Head of Design reviewing, and it launched together with the new website.

  • A separate path for each one. "Affiliates Log In" in the navigation is only for affiliates. Advertisers have their own page that takes them straight to the team.
  • One job per screen. The log-in page is just for logging in, with one clear link for new affiliates.
  • Three bite-sized steps instead of one long form. I split sign-up into Account, Publishing and Communication, with clear progress so people always know how much is left.
  • Labels that stay visible while you type, instead of placeholder text that disappears. Easier to read and better for accessibility.
  • Made for mobile too, with vertical steps and full-width buttons.

I also brought the new colours, typography and components into the platform itself, so the brand felt the same from the website to everyday use.

A Rollout made for Everyone

I rolled out new tools, assets, templates, and resources to make the transition simple, in shared folders easily reachable. A brand only works if people actually use it. So I made it easy for everyone, not just the design team:

  • Easy-to-follow brand guidelines.
  • Ready-to-use folders with all the new logos, icons and visual assets.
  • New templates for Google Slides and email signatures.

That way, anyone in the company could make a deck or send an email that looked right, without asking a designer.

The Results

The new brand was successfully rolled out across all platforms, ready to use and implement in a seamless and smooth way.

  • Design review cycles for marketing assets were shortened by an average of 60%.
  • Two brands and two websites became one brand, used across every touchpoint.
  • One website for a company with 50M+ monthly users and 200+ media partners.
  • One design system and set of guidelines, used by product, marketing and external communications.
  • A shared component library across all ClickOut products.
  • A clearer way into the affiliate platform, launched together with the website.

What I learned

Building the system was the easy part. The real work was making it easier to use than to ignore. The templates, the shared folders and the simple guidelines did more for consistency than any single component.

I also learned that a brand doesn't stop at the homepage. ClickOut was growing fast, so the site had to speak to future colleagues as much as to partners. And the way people get into the product is part of the brand too. Looking at the sign-up again showed me that "one way in for everyone" isn't always the simplest answer. Affiliates needed a quicker, lighter way to join. Advertisers needed to reach a person. Giving each group its own clear path worked better than forcing them through the same door.
